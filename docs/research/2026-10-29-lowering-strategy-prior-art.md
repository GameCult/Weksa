## 1. Fine-tuned lowerers (structure/data to text)

- AMR-to-text with pretrained seq2seq: T5-large about 45.8 BLEU plain, about 49.7 after task-adaptive training on silver data (Ribeiro et al., "Investigating Pretrained Language Models for Graph-to-Text Generation", arXiv 2007.08426); same paper varied training data 1%-100%: BART and T5 already good at 40% of data, T5 about 5.6 BLEU ahead of BART at 1% on WebNLG. [V (ss)]
- 2025 WiNLP (GPT4AMR) says top AMR-to-text models (AMRBART, BiBL) reach roughly 50 BLEU with a remaining quality gap; LLM paraphrase after AMR-to-text gave no automatic-metric gain but was preferred in human evaluation. [V (ss)] https://preview.aclanthology.org/setup/2025.winlp-main.2
- Few-shot/low-resource D2T: T5-base fine-tuned on 100 triple sets used as low-resource baseline (Cycle training, ACL 2023, arXiv 2305.14793); another summary reports neural D2T "high fidelity with about 300 annotated examples". [V (ss), second claim weakly sourced]
- AMR-to-text fine-tuned LLM vs prompted frontier LLM head-to-head on AMR 3.0 with BLEU or faithfulness: [GAP] (also noted as gap in the earlier prior-art file).
- Open LLMs zero-shot on D2T: Kasner and Dusek, ACL 2024 (arXiv 2401.10186): Llama 2, Mistral, Zephyr on QuintD (fresh API-derived records); human annotators and GPT-4 metric both flag over 80% of outputs as containing a semantic error (v1 said 80% human, 91% GPT-4). [V (ss)] No frontier-API or fine-tuned comparison in that paper as seen. [GAP]
- Faithfulness with model size: Mahapatra and Garain 2024 (arXiv 2407.14088), 12 models in T5/BART/OPT/BLOOM/Llama 2 families fine-tuned on five D2T sets incl. E2E, DART, WebNLG: larger fine-tuned models improve readability/informativeness, faithfulness (BARTScore) often decreases with size. [V (ss)]
- Error type: SPOR (arXiv 2405.10650) reports stronger fine-tuned models (Mistral-7B, Llama-2-13B) err mostly by omission on E2E; a Cambridge MLMI dissertation reports T5 outputs showed rare hallucination, many omissions. Cycle training reduced human-counted hallucination errors from 14.84 to 2.57 and factual errors from 8.05 to 0.49 (units as in paper; low-resource fine-tune baseline). [V (ss)]
- SCOPE (arXiv 2502.13674): faithfulness hallucination described as common in standard fine-tuned LLMs; self-supervised fix; notes WebNLG and E2E are of limited complexity. [V (ss)]
- Persona/style fine-tunes: no study found comparing LoRA/QLoRA persona-style tunes with strong prompting on voice fidelity. Nearest: (a) 2026 arXiv 2609.21857: two small open models fine-tuned on personality-labelled data were no better at role-playing personalities than baselines (authors note low inter-rater agreement); (b) PersonaEval (2508.10014): role-knowledge fine-tuning did not help role identification and may degrade it; (c) NeurIPS 2025 multi-turn RL with consistency rewards cut persona inconsistency by over 55% (simulated users). [V (ss)] [GAP: LoRA-specific voice-holding evidence]
- Style imitation: "Catch Me If You Can? Not Yet" (arXiv 2509.14543): few-shot beats zero-shot, 2 to 10 shots adds little, informal/everyday-author style imitation remains weak; no fine-tuning baseline run. "How Well Do LLMs Imitate Human Writing Style?": exemplar-based prompting and text completion are key; imitation remains lower-perplexity than human text. [V (ss)] [GAP: fine-tuned vs prompted on same authors]
- LoRA vs full fine-tuning: Biderman et al. 2024 (TMLR, arXiv 2405.09673): LoRA trails full FT in code/math in standard settings, forgets less outside target domain, sensitive to learning rate/target modules/scale more than rank. Follow-up 2410.21228: LoRA forgets less but "intruder dimensions" matter. [V (ss)]

## 2. Training data from the loop itself

- Back-translation, Sennrich/Haddow/Birch ACL 2016 (arXiv 1511.06709): synthetic source from target-side monolingual text mixed with real parallel data; +2.8-3.7 BLEU WMT15 en->de, +2.1-3.4 BLEU IWSLT14 tr->en. [V (ss)]
- Edunov et al. EMNLP 2018 (arXiv 1808.09381): sampled or noised-beam synthetic sources beat beam outputs by 1.7 BLEU average; beam still fine in very low-resource settings. Iterative back-translation (Hoang et al. 2018; Cotterell and Kreutzer 2018) cited second-hand only. [V (ss); Hoang itself GAP]
- D2T cycle training (ACL 2023, arXiv 2305.14793): trains text->data and data->text models in a cycle on unpaired data; fewer hallucination/factual errors than a 100-pair fine-tune (see §1). [V (ss)]
- Collapse: Shumailov et al., Nature 631:755 (2024): models trained recursively on generated data lose tails of the distribution and produce repetitive output, in LLMs, VAEs, GMMs. Whether mixing/accumulating real data prevents it: not confirmed from the sources seen. [V (ss)] [GAP: accumulate-vs-replace results]
- Round-trip leakage/inflation (same model both directions, consistency not accuracy): from prior pass, §2 of interlingua-lowering-prior-art.md. [V earlier pass]
- Specific to this loop: encoder output (packet) of an item and its English lowering form a synthetic pair where the English is the original; the weksa dataset note already marks such interlingua labels as derived from answer-key surface evidence. [V, weksa docs] [GAP: published work on training a generator on packets produced by an LLM encoder from the same targets; closest is back-translation/cycle training above]

## 4. Word-embedding mapping idea

- Mikolov, Le, Sutskever 2013 (arXiv 1309.4168) [V, PDF read]: linear translation matrix learned from a small dictionary (5K most frequent words) on monolingual word2vec spaces. Table 2 (WMT11): En->Sp P@1 33%, P@5 51%; Sp->En 35%/52%; En->Cz 27%/47%; Cz->En 23%/42% (readings from table layout; row/direction attribution partly ambiguous in the extracted text). The abstract's "almost 90% precision@5" applies to a confidence-filtered subset (about half of words in frequency ranks 5K-6K, distance threshold), not all words. Small monolingual training sets cut P@5 to about 25%. The paper says the method "was less useful for more [distant pairs]" (English/Czech, Vietnamese tested).
- MUSE, Conneau et al. ICLR 2018 (arXiv 1710.04087) [V, PDF read]: Table 1 P@1, unsupervised Adv-Refine-CSLS: en-es 81.7, es-en 83.3, en-fr 82.3, fr-en 82.1, en-de 74.0, de-en 72.2, en-ru 44.0, ru-en 59.1, en-zh 32.5, zh-en 31.4, en-eo 28.2, eo-en 25.6 (fastText on Wikipedia, 1,500 queries vs 200k targets). Word-by-word translation en<->eo BLEU 11.1 and 14.3; authors say meaning mostly conveyed with "simple errors" and fixable by an LM.
- Søgaard, Ruder, Vulic ACL 2018 (P18-1072): word-embedding spaces "far from isomorphic" even for close languages; unsupervised BDI degrades on morphologically rich non-dependent-marking languages, different domains/embedding algorithms; graph-similarity metric correlates near-perfectly with induction success; identical-word weak supervision helps. [V (ss)]
- VecMap (Artetxe et al.) as named; not read this pass. [B]
- Conlang with no corpus: [GAP] found no paper that places invented words at concept vectors or assigns an invented lexicon by clustering an embedding space. Nearest: Toki Pona word2vec study (Huang and Wu, arXiv 2606.17299, about 130 words: ANIMALS/COLORS form exclusive clusters, SHAPES/BODY/DIRECTION overlap) [V (ss)]; Cree lexicon classified semi-automatically with English word2vec vectors (AmericasNLP 2021) [V (ss)]; Spanish embedding-cluster "semantic domains" paper [V (ss)].
- Conlang LLM work: ConlangCrafter (arXiv 2508.06094) multi-stage LLM pipeline generating phonology, morphology, syntax, lexicon, translation, with self-refinement; evaluated on consistency and typological diversity. ConlangBench (arXiv 2608.03505): 21 conlangs, over 21M parallel pairs (Esperanto-dominated; 430K across the other 20), 321K vocab entries; models translate better into a posteriori conlangs (vocabulary from natural languages); models could learn all eight conlangs with sufficient parallel data. [V]
- MTOB (Tanzer et al., ICLR 2024, arXiv 2309.16575): Kalamang from one grammar book (~217K tokens) plus word list and sentences; best model about 44.7/45.8 chrF (kgv->en / en->kgv) vs human learner 51.6/57.0; follow-up 2508.06665 (GPT-4.1) finds parallel sentences alone beat full-grammar prompting and grammar alone fails on complex sentences. [V (ss)] Relevant as work on LLM + grammar documents for a language with no web corpus.
- Embedding inversion: vec2text (Morris et al., EMNLP 2023, arXiv 2310.06816): recovers 92% of 32-token inputs exactly from a GTR-base style embedding; on MSMARCO with ada-002: 83.4 BLEU/60.9% exact at 32 tokens, 55.0 BLEU/8.0% exact at 128 tokens; a 2025 reproduction notes length sensitivity. Inversion targets the same encoder's own English text; no conlang use found. [V (ss)] [GAP: inversion into non-English or invented surface]
- Sentence-embedding interlingua: SONAR (Meta, 200 languages text) used as concept space with a decoder in Large Concept Models (arXiv 2412.08821); SONAR reported better than LASER3 and LaBSE on xsim/xsim++. [V (ss)] LaBSE/LASER coverage details [B]. Weksa's current embedding: bge-m3 [V].
- "Nearest English cluster" as gloss vs fluent text: only indirect evidence: MUSE word-by-word en<->eo (above), Toki Pona wiki note that word-level glossing is the simplest approach and neural translators often err [V (ss)]. [GAP: measurement of nearest-cluster gloss vs fluent generation].

## 5. Hybrids

- Overgenerate-and-rank: Velldal (LOGON/Oslo) discriminative realization ranker trained on a few thousand treebank items beat a surface LM; Varges instance-based ranker over rule candidates; critique that overgeneration is expensive. [V (ss)]
- Neural ranking for word ordering in surface realization (pointwise/pairwise/listwise), five languages (2021). [V (ss)]
- Grammar-constrained decoding: Geng et al. EMNLP 2023 (arXiv 2305.13971): any CFG plugged into constrained beam search; constrained LMs beat unconstrained and sometimes finetuned models on IE/entity disambiguation with LLaMA. Outlines (Willard and Louf 2023, arXiv 2307.09702) regex/grammar guided generation; SynCode (2403.01632); Grammar-Aligned Decoding (2405.21047) argues grammar constraints distort the LM distribution. [V (ss)] Prior pass: Shin et al. 2021 (constrained decoding into a canonical sublanguage then deterministic map), Tam et al. 2024 (format constraints cost reasoning; two-step NL-then-convert keeps quality). [V earlier pass]
- GF + neural: Chalmers 2024 thesis builds a transformer parser to GF trees (prior pass). [GAP] no paper found on neural lexeme/register choice feeding a GF or SimpleNLG-style realiser, nor on LLM output constrained to a GF concrete syntax.
- Abstract Wikipedia/Ninai/Udiron (prior pass): symbolic realisers with lexeme lookup; no learned component. [V earlier pass]
